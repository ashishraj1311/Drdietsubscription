Dr Diet lets busy professionals in India subscribe to nutritionally-balanced meals delivered on a schedule that fits their life, with minimal planning effort. This document defines the product: who it serves, the end-to-end flow, what is built today, and the requirements and roadmap to a fully live, operationally-automated service. It reflects the current implementation — a complete functional app with a customer storefront, an admin console, and a real Supabase backend — and adds forward requirements for authentication, flexible scheduling, and automated delivery logistics.

1 · Product summary & goals

What it is

A mobile-first web app where a user discovers meal plans, builds a personalised plan through a guided wizard (or picks a curated plan), checks out with serviceability-aware delivery, pays, and manages an ongoing subscription from a dashboard. An operations-facing admin console manages customers, subscriptions, orders, menu, coupons, payments, reviews and support.

Goals

  • Take a first-time visitor from landing to a confirmed, paid subscription with zero dead ends.
  • Remove meal-planning effort while keeping nutrition aligned to a fitness goal.
  • Give customers real scheduling control — plan ahead or change day-to-day.
  • Automate fulfilment & protect margins — hands-off delivery at an optimised cost.

Non-goals (this release)

AI/photo-based body analysis · referral & rewards mechanics · native mobile apps (responsive web first) · in-house rider fleet or route optimisation (we integrate aggregators, not build logistics) · production-grade payment settlement (simulated until Phase 2).

2 · Primary persona

Raj Mehta · 25 · Corporate engineer & gym-goer

Works 8 hrs/day, 5 days/week; gyms 4×/week. Conscious about food for fitness, but unwilling to spend time planning meals. Trust bar is high, patience is low — he distrusts new brands and abandons any step that feels like too much commitment before he’s ready.

Wants: get fit · meals matched to his goal and macros · spend less time planning · flexibility to change or skip a day · a fast, password-free login. Fears: unclear billing, taste risk, “do they even deliver to me?”, being locked in.

5 · Authentication & login (requirements)

6 · Scheduling flexibility (requirements)

Customers must be able to plan ahead or stay fully day-to-day, without any change ever breaking the underlying subscription or billing. Orders mutate; the subscription and its cadence stay intact.

Pause / resumePause the whole subscription and resume later.Built
Pre-select meals aheadChoose the specific dish for each slot across a week or month in advance.Required
Edit meals dailyChange the dish for a given day up to a daily cut-off, for max flexibility.Required
Swap mealsSubstitute one dish for another (within diet/allergy rules) without disrupting the plan.Required

System rule: weekly/monthly plans, daily changes, skips, pauses and swaps must all operate on the order layer while the subscription record and billing cadence remain unbroken. A cut-off time governs how late an edit is allowed before kitchen lock.

7.2 · Optimised delivery pricing to protect margins Required · not started

Delivery must not erode meal margins. Today it is a flat fee with no optimisation; the requirement is a multi-aggregator, rate-aware routing layer.

  • Multiple aggregators — integrate several partners, no single locked-in provider.
  • Rate optimisation — compute cost by distance, order volume, and time of day.
  • Cheapest-reliable routing — route each order to the best-value dependable partner, per order.
Cost target: current ~₹120 for 7 km ≈ 48% of a ₹250 meal (unsustainable) → bring the effective 7 km cost to ₹60–80, depending on city and volume.

8 · Technical architecture

Stack

  • Next.js 16 (App Router) · React 19 · TypeScript
  • Tailwind CSS v4 — CSS-first theming via @theme tokens
  • Fonts: Barlow + Baloo 2 (accent stand-in) · Hosting: Vercel

The swappable data seam

All stateful operations go through a single DrDietApi interface. A localStorage mock is the zero-config default; the Supabase adapter activates automatically when environment keys are present — no UI code changes. The same seam is where delivery-partner and payment providers plug in.

Backend & security

  • Supabase Postgres, schema mirrors the data model (INR-only, no beef).
  • Row-Level Security: catalog world-readable; users access only their own data.
  • Auth: one-time code (OTP) by email or phone only (see §5).

Planned integration surfaces

Messaging: SMTP (Resend) for email codes + verified domain; SMS provider for phone codes. Payments: Razorpay (order + webhook, UPI Autopay). Delivery: a routing service fanning out to multiple aggregator APIs, with webhooks for tracking & proof-of-delivery.

10 · Success criteria & metrics

Acceptance

  • Home → confirmed paid subscription with no dead-end click.
  • A user signs in with an emailed/texted OTP and a purchase persists to the backend, visible across devices and in admin.
  • A customer can pre-plan a week and also change/skip/swap a single day without breaking billing.
  • An order auto-allocates a rider and captures proof of delivery with no manual step.

Product & ops metrics

  • OTP request → verify success rate; time-to-sign-in
  • Builder completion & checkout conversion; Trial → paid
  • Payment success rate; involuntary churn from failed renewals
  • Pause/skip/swap rate, MRR; delivery cost per order & % of meal price; on-time & POD rate

12 · Open items & risks

  • OTP delivery not live — login UX is built, but email codes need custom SMTP (+ verified domain) and phone codes need an SMS provider before anyone can actually sign in on production (§5).
  • Payments are simulated — no money moves until Razorpay (Phase 2).
  • Meal-level flexibility missing — pre-select / daily edit / swap not built; meals are auto-assigned today (§6).
  • No delivery automation — order status is set manually in admin; no aggregator API, tracking, or POD (§7.1).
  • Delivery cost unoptimised — flat fee, no multi-aggregator routing; ~48% of meal price today vs. ₹60–80 target (§7.2).
